# Principles

Five, in priority order. When two collide, the earlier one wins.

## 1. State the evidence
Every claim can say how well it is supported. The evidence ladder exists so a
reader never has to guess whether a sentence is a metaanalysis or an opinion.
Absence is meaningful: most claims carry no grade, which is what makes a
grade a signal. Never infer a grade she did not state.

## 2. Nothing without a source
No statement about her, her work, or a study that does not trace to a source.
An empty collection is announced in one true sentence, not filled with a
placeholder. A missing photograph is a missing photograph, not a stock
stand-in that stays.

## 3. One voice, first person
She speaks. Not "we", not a biography. Headings are full-sentence claims in
sentence case. Czech typography rules are enforced, not hoped for. The same
voice on a slide, a reel, a web page and a handout.

## 4. Blue is the chrome; colour is information
One accent does all the furniture. Petrol and violet appear only as topic
identity, always beside the topic's name in text. On a chart, a second colour
is sand and only when two curves must be told apart. A third hue is a second
chart.

## 5. Flat at rest
Nothing carries a shadow, a lift or a motion until someone touches it or
scrolls to it. Photographs pass through the duotone so they sit in the same
family as the bands beside them. Whitespace does the separating; borders are
a hairline.

## Corollaries that come up constantly
- A heading that is already a claim gets no eyebrow.
- Text over a photograph needs a real background-colour scrim.
- The commercial ask appears once, after the proof.
- Two modes of imagery exist (Public duotone, Clinical natural) and are never
  mixed in one piece.
